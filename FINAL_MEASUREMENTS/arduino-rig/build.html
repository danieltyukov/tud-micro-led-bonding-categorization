Arduino I-V rig: the breadboard, hole for hole

This is the wiring in 2_ARDUINO_IV.md drawn out. Nothing is added and nothing is left out: 11 male-male jumpers, 2 female-male, 7 resistors, 3 caps, one Arduino UNO, one breadboard. Rail holes follow the column numbers: first one under column 3, then five in a row, a one-hole gap, five more, and so on.

Count before you power up

12male-male jumpers: 6 bank, A0, A1, A2, A3, GND, 1 link
2female-male jumpers, the only wires leaving the board
7resistors: six in the bank, one 100 Ω sense
3capacitors, 68 nF each: C1 rail to rail, C2 and C3 on RETURN
  1. Six bank resistors: one leg in row A at columns 14, 16, 18, 20, 22, 24, the other leg in the FORCE rail one column to the right.
  2. Jumper D2–D7 into row E of each bank column: D7 to E14, up to D2 at E24.
  3. Link E4 to E8. All ten of those holes are now RETURN.
  4. 100 Ω from B8 down to the GND rail at column 11.
  5. C2 from A4 and C3 from A8 down to the GND rail; C1 from FORCE col 11 across to GND col 13.
  6. Jumper UNO GND to the GND rail at column 3. No second ground wire anywhere.
  7. Jumper A0 to D4, A2 to D8, A1 to the FORCE rail at column 6, and A3 to the GND rail at column 10.
  8. Two female-male jumpers out to the die: FORCE to pin 4n−2, RETURN C4 to the cathode pin of the colour you are sweeping.

To move to the next channel, move only those last two jumpers. Everything else stays put for the whole session.

Why A3 is there

Step 3 was run on a 100 Ω dummy and the rig failed it. A pure resistor must fit v_die = i·R through the origin. It fitted 99.32 Ω but with a +13.8 mV intercept, which is not something a resistor can do.

Constant in millivolts across a 30× current range, so it is an additive ADC offset of about 10 mV, not a resistance or reference error, which would both scale with current. A diagnostic sweeping ADC prescaler (/32, /64, /128) against discarding a conversion after each mux change — six combinations — gave the same offset every time, so it is neither clock speed nor mux settling.

The offset cancels in v_die, because that is a difference of two channels. It does not cancel in the current, which was an absolute voltage. At the bottom of the sweep the current was wrong by 26 %.

A3 makes the current a difference too. It taps the GND rail in the same five-hole group as the 100 Ω's lower leg, and the sketch computes i = (v_A2 − v_A3) / R_SENSE. The offset subtracts out exactly, and the IR drop in the single GND jumper leaves the measurement with it.

Also found: 2.9 Ω in the E4–E8 link

A0 and A2 are the same node by design and must read identically. A0 read high by an amount strictly proportional to current, 2.9 ± 0.2 Ω across levels 2 to 32. That is contact resistance in the link jumper or its sockets. In this topology it sits outside both the v_die loop and the current tap, so it is not corrupting R_s, but it is thirty times the bond resistances being measured and a joint that loose will not stay put. Reseat it.

Capacitor value

68 nF for all three. Anything from about 47 to 100 nF works and the tolerance does not matter, since nothing in the measurement depends on the value. Put the smallest of the three on C1, which is the only one whose settling is tight.

The slow node is FORCE with only the 10 kΩ branch on, at the bottom of the sweep, charging through t = R·C·ln(5/(5−V_F)). The sketch allows SETTLE_MS = 2, so 100 nF is the ceiling. Below about 30 nF the ADC's 14 pF sample-and-hold starts stealing a visible fraction of the charge on every one of the 256 oversampled reads.

§4.5 of the background doc says up to 1 µF. It cannot.

At 1 µF a blue channel needs 9.2 ms to reach V_F on the lowest current step, against a 2 ms settle. The ADC would read a node still on its way up, the fit would see too little voltage at low current, and that lands squarely on R_s as series resistance that is not there. The same section's own worked example (0.7 ms at 68 nF, comfortably inside 2 ms) is the reasoning that rules out its own upper bound.

Two things in the doc that do not agree

The Build block still lists the old resistor values

The parts table in 2_ARDUINO_IV.md says 10 k, 5.1 k, 2 k, 1 k, 330 Ω, 220 Ω, and the current table underneath it (0.32 / 0.62 / 1.53 / 2.93 / 7.49 / 10.06 mA) is computed from exactly those. The ASCII Build block a few lines lower still says 4k7, 2k2 and 470, carried over from measurements/ARDUINO_IV_RIG.md. The drawing uses the parts table. The ASCII block is the one that needs editing.

A0 and A2 land on the same node, so this is not Kelvin sensing

Section 4.2 of ARDUINO_IV_RIG.md puts A1 and A0 on the Tier-1 probe pads so that jumper wire and header contact resistance sit outside the sense loop. Round 2 instead takes both from the breadboard, which folds both female-male jumpers and both header contacts back into V(A1) − V(A0). That is a few tens to a couple of hundred mΩ, the same size as the bonds you are chasing.

It is not fatal, and it is the reason the plan already asks for seat2 and seat3 repeats: the wiring term is what re-seating changes. But quote R_s from this rig as die + wiring, and treat the seat-to-seat spread as the error bar on the wiring part rather than as noise.
